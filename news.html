Lab News

Stay updated with the latest news, achievements, and events from the Nussear Lab.

October 2024

New Graduate Student Joins the Lab

We are excited to welcome our newest graduate student who will be working on spatial ecology of sagebrush ecosystems. They bring expertise in remote sensing and GIS analysis that will greatly enhance our research capabilities.

September 2024

Grant Awarded for Desert Tortoise Research

The lab has been awarded a three-year grant from the National Science Foundation to study the impacts of climate change on desert tortoise populations. This funding will support field research, spatial modeling, and graduate student training.

August 2024

Publication in Ecological Applications

Our latest research on climate-driven changes in desert tortoise habitat suitability has been published in Ecological Applications. The study provides important insights for conservation planning in the Mojave Desert.

July 2024

Successful Field Season in the Great Basin

The lab completed an intensive field season surveying wildlife and habitat across the Great Basin. We collected valuable data on species distributions, vegetation patterns, and environmental conditions that will support multiple ongoing research projects.

June 2024

Graduate Student Receives Best Paper Award

Congratulations to our graduate student for receiving the Best Student Paper Award at the annual Wildlife Society conference. The award-winning paper presented novel approaches to modeling wildlife connectivity in fragmented landscapes.

May 2024

Collaboration with Nevada Department of Wildlife

The lab has initiated a new collaboration with the Nevada Department of Wildlife to assess habitat corridors across the state. This partnership will combine our research expertise with the agency's management needs to develop practical conservation solutions.

April 2024

Workshop on Conservation Planning

Dr. Nussear led a workshop on climate-informed conservation planning for state and federal agency biologists. The workshop covered spatial analysis techniques, climate modeling, and strategies for incorporating uncertainty into conservation decisions.

March 2024

PhD Student Successfully Defends Dissertation

Congratulations to our PhD student on successfully defending their dissertation on reptile responses to climate change. They will be joining a federal research agency as a wildlife biologist. We are proud of their accomplishments and wish them well in their new position.
