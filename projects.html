Research Projects

Explore our ongoing and completed research projects focused on conservation biology, ecology, and wildlife management.

Current Projects

Desert Tortoise Conservation

Status: Ongoing

Funding: National Science Foundation

This project investigates the effects of climate change on desert tortoise populations in the Mojave Desert. We use spatial modeling, field surveys, and physiological studies to understand how changing temperatures and precipitation patterns affect tortoise behavior, survival, and reproduction. Our findings inform conservation strategies and habitat management plans.

Team: Dr. Kenneth Nussear, Graduate Students

Spatial Ecology of Sagebrush Ecosystems

Status: Ongoing

Funding: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service

Examining the spatial patterns of sagebrush ecosystems and their wildlife communities. This research combines remote sensing, GIS analysis, and field data to understand how landscape structure affects species distributions and ecosystem processes. Results guide habitat restoration and management efforts across the Great Basin.

Team: Dr. Kenneth Nussear, Research Scientists

Wildlife Connectivity and Habitat Corridors

Status: Ongoing

Funding: Nevada Department of Wildlife

Identifying critical habitat corridors and assessing landscape connectivity for wildlife movement in Nevada. Using advanced spatial analysis and movement ecology techniques, we map connectivity patterns and prioritize areas for conservation. This work supports landscape-level conservation planning and helps mitigate habitat fragmentation.

Team: Dr. Kenneth Nussear, Graduate Students, Collaborators

Completed Projects

Climate Change Impacts on Reptile Distributions

Status: Completed (2020-2023)

Funding: USGS

Investigated how climate change affects the distribution of reptile species in the southwestern United States. Developed predictive models to forecast future distribution changes and identify climate refugia. Findings have been incorporated into regional conservation planning efforts.

Team: Dr. Kenneth Nussear, Former Graduate Students
